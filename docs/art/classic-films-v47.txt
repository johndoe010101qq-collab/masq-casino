Coinflip uses the accepted face to choose matching recorded physics. It cuts
from the wide shot to the close shot at frame10, keeping the same absolute film

720×960), not redrawn coins. Both138-frame variants hold the correct final
face on the felt. Their physical spins differ from release onward: the old
procedural shared-prefix reveal rule is no longer a visual claim for these
recorded coin throws. Accepted RNG, money, duration and payout are unchanged.
The source close-up briefly clips the bright mask at the apex; no new lighting
or character reconstruction was introduced to hide this source limitation.

Roulette keeps the filmed operator and outer bowl. A raster rotor extracted
from the supplied top-down plate rotates its pockets, frets and all37 actual
numbers together, opposite the post-release ball. Source frame36 already omits its
ball, so handoff starts from physics.release.pos/velocity, not placeholder
frame36 object coordinates. The renderer checks the decoded release frame to
avoid two balls while a seek decodes. All37 outcomes use their accepted pocket;
no arbitrary pocket from the supplied preview movie is used. The common orbit
does not read the pocket before the late accepted-result reveal.
The camera moves with the complete film and projection together. The final
actual pocket and number are accented; no invented near-miss is introduced.

Upgrader projects text and progress onto the supplied CRT homography. The
terminal stays READY until the filmed activation mark, then runs the common
check. Success/failure is revealed only at the existing .92 boundary. Essential
stake, target and payout remain in the normal interface outside the tiny screen.

Plinko v47 uses exact source tracks, native mobile calibration, label rectangles
and whole-character after-clips that continue from handoff19. Accepted balls
remain independent and unlimited. The full-size balls clear the actual square
pin caps before descending; there is no per-ball animation/admission queue.
See public/plinko-dealer-v47/source-metadata/README.txt for calibration details.

ClassicFilmStage uses two native media buffers. The existing financial runner
owns public progress; paused action films seek that progress, and idle films
play natively. Media errors, rejected play promises, skip, reduced motion,
hidden tabs and decoded-frame callbacks never debit or settle any round.
A previous decoded frame stays visible until its replacement is decoded.
Actions map inclusive progress0..1 to frame0..last, not the empty video endpoint.
